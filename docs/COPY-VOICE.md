# Copy voice — what the words are allowed to claim

Written after three passes at a 404 page. The rules are about epistemics as
much as tone: almost every correction was a sentence claiming something the
page could not know.

## The rules

1. **State what is.** "Nothing here" is true. "A typo" is a guess about a
   stranger's hands wearing the costume of a fact.
2. **Hedge the guess, not the fact.** One "maybe" is welcome. Three make the
   page anxious.
3. **No invented will.** "Something took a wrong turn" gives an error volition,
   which then demands a moral judgment nobody can make.
4. **No claimed cause.** "Isn't working right now" asserts knowledge of the
   present. "Something seems up" admits the same uncertainty the reader has.
5. **No promised outcome.** "Give it another go in a moment" promises the next
   minute and the reader's patience.
6. **No instructions.** A button is an offer. The sentence beside it is not a
   supervisor.
7. **No fault, in either direction.** "Not your fault" presupposes fault and
   hands out absolution nobody asked for.
8. **No person.** Not "you" (presumptuous), not "we" (a relationship the
   moment has not earned). Say "the list", "the page", "the link".
9. **No idiom.** "No worries", "one of those days" — each is one postcode's
   shorthand pretending to be universal. The reader may be twelve, or reading
   a second language.
10. **No first person.** There is no staff behind the curtain of a one-person
    app, and "we" implies an institution that does not exist.
11. **Short.** Two words of headline beats five. Cut the sentence that explains
    the sentence.
12. **Blame the state, never the reader or the site.**

## Worked example

Before: "We looked, but there's nothing here. The link may have a typo, or it
moved. No worries — your lists are right where you left them."

After:

> **Uh-oh.**
> Nothing here. Maybe later.

Same reassurance, none of the claims. "Maybe later" is the whole philosophy:
honest about not knowing, warm about it anyway.
